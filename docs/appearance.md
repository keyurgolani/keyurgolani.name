# Appearance

How a portfolio looks is four independent choices, plus a display mode (#19): the **variant** composes the content and designs its own elements from the tokens, the **design language** sets materials, the geometry tokens, states, and timing, the **color scheme** sets color, and the **typography** profile sets the text. Changing one never changes the others. This page is the contract between them, for anyone building a variant or a pack, and the format a third-party pack ships in.

`@portfolio/appearance` holds it: the token contract, the pack manifests and their compiler, the resolver, the presentation boundary, and the shared primitives.

## Who owns what

| Axis            | Owns                                                                                                                               | Never                                                                                 |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Variant         | Information architecture, layout, emphasis, the reading flow, and the design of its own components and elements, from the tokens   | Color values, fonts, materials, focus and state colors, timing, the primitives' parts |
| Design language | Materials, the geometry tokens (borders, shadows, radii), density, focus, interaction states, the primitives' parts, icons, timing | Color values, type, layout, a composition's choice among its tokens                   |
| Color scheme    | Every semantic color, in each display mode                                                                                         | Anything but color                                                                    |
| Typography      | Text roles, font files, scale, rhythm, reading measures, and script fallbacks                                                      | Color, layout                                                                         |

The display mode, `system`, `light`, `dark`, or `high-contrast`, is an environment, not a scheme: every scheme renders in it, or says which modes it lacks. Reduced motion, forced colors, and reduced transparency override every creative choice: whatever a pack or a variant sets, the accessibility layer and the visitor's operating system win. High contrast and print drop the language's texture, as forced colors does, since every high-contrast set draws its borders, a texture's ink among them, in the text color. Forced colors also set every language token that moves a part, such as a press's `translate()`, and every shadow token to `none`, since a language's stylesheet can't test forced colors itself.

## Surface pairings

A composition may place a band on two surfaces besides the canvas and `surface-*`:

- **The inverse pair:** a ground of `--color-text-primary` with `--color-text-inverse` ink. `text-inverse` is the canvas, so the pair is exactly as legible as text on the canvas.
- **The matte pair:** `--color-scrim` with `--color-on-scrim` ink.
- **The accent pair:** `--color-accent` with `--color-on-accent` ink, 4.5:1 or more at body size in every shipping scheme, authored ones included (warm-parchment's light set is the closest, at 4.75:1).
- **The emphasis pair:** `--color-emphasis` with `--color-on-emphasis` ink. `on-emphasis` is black or white, whichever contrasts more, so it reaches 4.58:1 or more on any emphasis.
- **The lit surface:** `--color-surface-lit`, lighter than `surface-prominent` in both base modes, with `--color-text-primary` ink at 4.95:1 or more. It's the canvas in a high-contrast set.

`apps/web/scripts/surface-pairings.test.ts` holds the inverse and matte pairs to 4.5:1, and 7:1 in a high-contrast set, and the other three to their ratios, in every shipping scheme and every set it renders. In high contrast the scrim can match the canvas, so a frame on the matte draws a `var(--ui-border-card)` edge in `--color-border-default` there. Secondary ink on a band is a `color-mix(in oklab, …)` of two role tokens, and the composition that mixes it proves its ratio in its own unit test.

Mark a band on the accent, inverse, or emphasis pair with `data-ui-surface="accent"`, `"inverse"`, or `"emphasis"`, and the accessibility layer gives everything inside it the pair's focus color and selection: `--color-focus` reads `on-accent`, `focus-inverse`, or `on-emphasis`, and selected text is the ground's color on that ink (on the inverse pair, `text-primary` on `text-inverse`). `focus-inverse` is the ring on a `text-primary` ground. A scheme pack derives it to 4.5:1 or more, as it derives `focus` (below), and a generator at version 1 to 3.5:1; the gate holds every set to 3.5:1 or more, above the 3:1 non-text floor so a ring on a dark scheme's cream inverse ground doesn't read soft, and to 4.5:1 in high contrast. So a control focused on the band rings legibly without a `--color-focus` of the composition's own, which the source audit refuses anyway. Mark the band, not a control: the band's own ring, drawn outside it, takes the pair too. Nested regions select by the order of the rules, not by the nearest region: text in an accent sticker inside an inverse band selects in the inverse pair, which is legible, since each rule sets a whole pair, but not the sticker's own (the backlog has the fix).

This amends #19's first split, where the design language owned geometry, states, and density. The owner defined the variant axis as deciding "component and element design as well as layout": the packs keep every value, and the composition chooses among them for its own elements ([Variants](variants.md#styling-conventions)).

## The selection

A site document's `appearance` names all five, and `visitorControls` says, per axis, whether a visitor may change it (`locked` or `selectable`):

```yaml
appearance:
  variant: editorial-evidence
  designLanguage: platform-neutral
  colorScheme: warm-parchment
  typography: humanist-ui
  displayMode: system
```

Those five are the platform's defaults, and the first-party fallbacks: a selection that names a pack this build doesn't register renders its axis's fallback, the boundary says so in `data-appearance-fallback`, the owner gets a diagnostic, and readiness reports degraded. Nothing claims the missing pack is active. [Configuration](configuration.md#appearance) has the fields; the demo profile makes every axis selectable in the visitor's own browser.

A visitor changes a selectable axis with the appearance control the variant places in its `VisitorControlSlot`. It's a form that works without JavaScript, and with it the page restyles in place.

## Resolution and layers

The resolver turns the published selection, the visitor's choice where an axis allows one, and the environment (the OS's color scheme and contrast preferences, and a preview frame's override) into one resolved appearance per boundary. Every layered stylesheet starts with the same statement, so the first one a page loads fixes the order and no later one can change it:

```css
@layer reset, scheme, language, typography, variant-layout, utilities, accessibility;
```

Lowest first: the platform's resets, the color scheme, the design language, the typography, the variant's layout, the utilities no pack or variant may undo, such as an outline focus ring and a control's touch target, and the accessibility overrides, which win over everything. A pack's stylesheet compiles into its own layer, and a variant's into `variant-layout`, so a variant designs its own elements from the tokens but never restyles a pack's primitive parts.

## The boundary

There's no global appearance state on `<html>`, which keeps only `lang` and `dir`. Appearance applies at a **presentation boundary**, an element the host renders with the resolved selection on it, and every token is set on that element:

```html
<div
  data-appearance-boundary="document"
  data-variant="editorial-evidence"
  data-design-language="platform-neutral"
  data-color-scheme="warm-parchment"
  data-typography="humanist-ui"
  data-display-mode="light"
  data-mode-locked="false"
  data-contrast="system"
  data-motion="full"
  data-enhancement="on"
></div>
```

- `document` is the page's one boundary, around its variant, with the skip link first and ids unprefixed.
- `plate` previews another selection inside a page, such as a catalog card: inert, contained, `data-motion="static"`, `data-enhancement="off"`, and with its own id prefix.

Boundaries nest and sit side by side without leaking style, state, ids, fonts, or events into each other, which is how Studio compares two selections on one page. `data-contrast` is `system`, or `more` when the visitor asks for more contrast. `data-motion` is `full`, `reduced` (the site's `reduce` policy, or a preview's reduced environment), or `static` (a plate, or a paused page), and the motion tokens also follow the OS setting from first paint. Stop motion of your own under `[data-variant='<id>']:is([data-motion='reduced'], [data-motion='static'])`. A document boundary's scheme and language also give the root's `::view-transition` their `--color-*` and `--ui-motion-*` tokens, outside print, since the view-transition pseudo-elements inherit from `<html>`, not the boundary (K-4 (a)); nothing in the document's own tree gets them.

## Tokens

Token contract 1 is every custom property the packs define, under three prefixes. A variant reads them with `var()`; it defines no token of its own but `--layout-*`.

- **`--color-<role>`**, from the color scheme: `canvas`; `surface-subtle`, `surface-default`, `surface-prominent`, `surface-overlay`; `text-primary`, `text-secondary`, `text-muted`, `text-inverse`; `border-subtle`, `border-default`, `border-strong`; `accent`, `accent-hover`, `accent-active`, `accent-text`, `on-accent`; `focus`, `selection`; `positive`, `warning`, `danger`, `information`, `on-status`; `data-categorical-1` to `-6`, `data-sequential-start`, `-middle`, `-end`; `signature-start`, `signature-end`, `emphasis`; `scrim`, `on-scrim`; and `shadow`; and `on-emphasis`, `focus-inverse`, and `surface-lit`, which K-2 added and every derivation works out from the others. Additive roles stay at token contract 1: `tokenContract` is still `1`, and no minor names them.
- **`--ui-<key>`**, from the design language: the surface material and opacity; radii for controls, cards, overlays, media, and badges; borders for controls, cards, and dividers; card, overlay, raised, and inset shadows; backdrop blur; control height and control and surface spacing; the focus width, offset, and style; hover, active, selected, disabled, loading, and validation states; icon stroke and fill; the motion durations (`motion-fast`, `motion-standard`, `motion-slow`), easing, and enter, exit, and emphasis motions; texture; and the signature fill and emphasis glow. Borders and shadows reach color only through `var(--color-*)`.
- **`--type-<role>-<field>`**, from the typography profile, for each role, `display`, `headline`, `title`, `body`, `body-compact`, `label`, `caption`, `quote`, `code`, and `numeric`, and each field, `font-family`, `font-size`, `font-weight`, `line-height`, `letter-spacing`, and `font-variation-settings`; and `--type-measure-body` and `--type-measure-compact`, the reading widths, in em: the profile's counts of characters, which the compiler resolves per face.

Set text in a role with `data-type-role="<role>"` on the element, and the platform stylesheet, which carries the primitives' rules, typesets it from those tokens. Under reduced motion the state transforms read `none`, entrances and exits fade, the emphasis motion is `none`, and `--ui-motion-standard` and `--ui-motion-slow` read the language's `--ui-motion-fast`, which the pack gates hold to the 200 ms `quality/thresholds.json` allows; only `static`, a plate or a paused page, zeroes them. So a variant that takes its timing and movement from the tokens needs no motion query of its own, and a keyframe animation of its own stops under `[data-variant='<id>']:is([data-motion='reduced'], [data-motion='static'])`.

## Shared scales

The platform stylesheet also declares the scales the redesigned compositions build on, from `@portfolio/appearance`'s `scales.ts`. Each fluid value is a `clamp()` between two rem lengths with a rem-plus-vw preferred term, growing from 360 px to 1240 px wide and held beyond, and its largest is at most 2.5 times its smallest, so zoom always reaches twice its size. The kit's shared renderers read the space scale, the rhythm, and the text width; each composition moves onto the rest with its redesign.

- **`--space-<step>`:** `3xs`, `2xs`, `xs`, `s`, `m`, `l`, `xl`, `2xl`, and `3xl`, a quarter to six times a base of 18 px at 360 px and 20 px at 1240 px. Adjacent steps are at least 25% apart at every width. The pairs `--space-s-l`, `--space-l-2xl`, and `--space-xl-3xl` run from the first step's smallest to the second's largest, for gutters and section padding. Only the platform declares them, so there's one scale.
- **`--rhythm-<role>`:** the section rhythm on the same base, by the part's role. `chapter` (126 to 192 px) opens a region, `section` (81 to 128 px) follows another section, `group` (40.5 to 64 px) joins a section to the one it belongs with, `item` (24 to 40 px) separates a list's entries, and `head` (18 to 20 px) sits below a heading. Adjacent roles are at least 25% apart at every width. The parent sets them: the kit's `pf-stack` puts `section` between its children, and `chapter` or `group` before a child whose `data-rhythm` says so, which `sectionRhythms()` in `@portfolio/kit/sections` works out from each section's kind.
- **`--type-step-<n>`,** from `-1` to `7`: a modular scale on the same base, 1.175 at 360 px and 1.25 at 1240 px, with step 7 under the 6rem display ceiling, `--type-display-ceiling`. `--type-<role>-fluid-size` names each role's step, and `--type-<role>-size-adjust` its `font-size-adjust`, 0.5, so a family swapped in keeps the x-height the layout was set with. A typography pack may give a role its own aspect, from 0.45 to 0.56, as the role's optional `font-size-adjust` field, for a face whose x-height is far from half its size: Newsreader's 0.426 would otherwise render 1.174 times as large. The compiler writes it into the role's `--type-<role>-size-adjust` in the typography layer, which wins over the platform's; a role that sets none keeps 0.5. A role's size on the page is still its profile's `--type-<role>-font-size`.
- **`--layout-grid-columns`, `-gutter`, and `-margin`,** 12, `var(--space-s-l)`, and `var(--space-s-l)`; and the named widths **`--layout-width-text`, `-outset`, `-page`, and `-screen`**: the profile's body measure, the measure plus `2xl` on each side, 80rem, and the full width of whatever holds the element; and **`--layout-measure-scale`**, 1, what the profile's measures are multiplied by. They're registered with `@property`, so each computes once, on the boundary: a text width in `ch` is the boundary's body text's, whatever text reads it. A composition that applies `font-size-adjust` to its body text sets the measure scale, in its root rule, to `calc(var(--type-body-size-adjust) / var(--type-body-aspect))`, as Spatial Bento does, so the measures count characters as they render (R11.1).
- **`--layout-motion-curve`, `-feedback`, and `-scene`:** what the composition wants, one easing and two durations: the language's `--ui-motion-easing`, 200 ms, and 800 ms unless it sets its own. Its feedback duration is 150 to 300 ms, its scene duration 600 to 1000 ms, and its curve the language's easing or a `cubic-bezier()` that doesn't overshoot, which the source audit holds.
- **`--layout-motion-ambient`:** one cycle of a loop that runs while the page shows, such as a sky's drift, a bead's travel, or blobs that merge: 40 s unless the composition sets its own, from 20 to 60 s, which the source audit holds.
- **`--motion-curve`, `--motion-feedback`, and `--motion-scene`:** what a composition's rules read for motion, which the platform works out on the boundary: the curve as set, and each duration no longer than 100 times the language's `--ui-motion-fast`, so a still language, at 0.01 ms, keeps every composition still. Under reduced motion both durations are `--ui-motion-fast`, and when paused or printed `0s`.
- **`--motion-ambient` and `--motion-ambient-state`:** what an ambient loop reads, as its duration and its `animation-play-state`: `animation: <id>-drift var(--motion-ambient) linear infinite; animation-play-state: var(--motion-ambient-state)`. The cycle is the slot as set, which no language caps. Only the pause, reduced motion, and a static page stop it: the state is `running`, and `paused` under the OS's or the boundary's reduced motion, `data-motion="static"`, print, and inside a runtime root that isn't active (`data-activity="paused"`: the visitor's pause, a hidden tab, or offscreen). A page gated from the start (a plate, print, or reduced motion at load) shows the loop's first frame; a pause mid-run holds whatever frame it's on, so every frame has to read as a still, or the composition swaps in its still under `data-activity="paused"`. The pause reaches a loop through the state alone: a one-shot entrance below a paused root plays to its end, never frozen part-way. The source audit's `motion-gate` rule holds every infinite loop in a composition's stylesheet to the state, under the kit runtime's `[data-kit-runtime]` gate too. Put the loop inside an element attached to `useEnhancementRuntime()`'s `attachRoot`, or the visitor's pause can't reach it. Each infinite loop counts toward the composition's `animation` budget ([Variants](variants.md#motion)).

A composition sets the grid, the widths, and the motion slots in its root rule, `[data-variant='<id>']`, alone (the root may carry attribute selectors of the composition's own, such as `[data-tf-field='quiet']`, never one the platform sets, which `isPlatformAttribute()` in `boundary.ts` names, kept to what `boundaryAttributes()` sets by a test), and never from code, so they compute on the boundary. A composition's own root attribute can only arrive from its code after the first paint, so the first paint uses the root rule's own values; the source audit refuses them anywhere else.

Motion has two owners. The composition owns choreography: its reveals, scene and chapter changes, navigation transitions, and its own interactions, timed by `--motion-*`. The design language owns effect character: its `--ui-motion-*` values time the primitives' state transitions, hover, focus, and press on the controls it styles, and a still language keeps the composition still through the cap above. So a composition never times a primitive's states, and never puts a language's durations into its choreography.

## The shared primitives

Fifteen components own behavior and semantics, and the packs style them through stable `data-ui-slot` parts: Action, Surface, Navigation, Field, Select, Checkbox, Radio, Badge, Tabs, Disclosure, Dialog, Tooltip, Toast, Media, and DataDisplay, with the landmarks and `SkipLink` beside them. Import them from `@portfolio/appearance/primitives`. Each keeps its keyboard, touch, focus, and assistive technology behavior under every design language; a variant chooses which one goes where and with what emphasis (`primary`, `secondary`, or `quiet` for an action; `subtle`, `default`, `prominent`, or `overlay` for a surface; and a status, always a word too, for a badge), and never restyles one. Native elements come first: a Disclosure is a `details`, an Action a `button` or a link.

The static ones render on the server and in a plate alike: Action, Surface, Badge, Disclosure, Field, Checkbox, Radio, Media, DataDisplay, and Toast. Tabs, Dialog, Tooltip, ToastRegion, Select, and the landmarks are client components that read their boundary, and attach nothing inside a plate.

## Packs

A pack is one design language, color scheme, or typography profile. A package holds one or more of one kind, and declares which in its `package.json`:

```json
{
  "name": "@acme/appearance-tide",
  "version": "1.0.0",
  "portfolio": { "kind": "color-scheme", "packs": "./packs" }
}
```

`kind` is `design-language`, `color-scheme`, or `typography`, and `packs` is a directory with one folder per pack, each holding a `manifest.json` and any files it names. A pack package ships no CSS outside what its manifest declares, since the compiler writes every rule, and no JavaScript at all: a package that exports a script, or a pack directory holding a `.js`, `.mjs`, `.ts`, `.wasm`, `.html`, or `.svg` file, stops the build.

A Research record, a reviewed candidate that doesn't ship, is `research/<id>.json` beside `packs/`: its `id`, `name`, `kind`, `tier: "research"`, `source` (a `name`, and a `url` or `null`), what's known of its `license` (`unverified` until someone checks), `researchedAt`, the `reason` it doesn't ship, and optionally a palette's `anchors`. Registry generation never reads `research/`, so a record is never registered, selectable, published, exported, or bundled, and selecting its id is `appearance.unavailable`. The build job's `pnpm check:catalog-build` fails when a Research id reaches the generated modules, `ASSET_MANIFEST`, any `.next/static` chunk, or the build output the image ships. Promoting one is its own owner-approved pull request that turns it into a pack.

### Every pack

Each manifest is strict, so a key the contract doesn't define is an error, never ignored, and each error names the pack, its axis, and the field.

Any other license keeps a valid pack out of the build: registry generation leaves it out, serves none of its files, warns with the license it found, and selecting it renders its axis's fallback with that reason.

### A color scheme

Its colors come one of three ways, and only one:

- `light` and `dark`, each a set with every color role, as six-digit lowercase hex, except that `on-emphasis`, `focus-inverse`, and `surface-lit` may be left out, all three or none: the compiler works them out from the set's other roles, as every derivation does. A set that gives them gives a `surface-lit` lighter than `surface-prominent`, and in a high-contrast set its canvas;
- `anchors` with a `derivation`: per mode, a `canvas`, `text`, `accent`, and `secondary`, and for `anchor-v2` an optional `emphasis` and `categorical`, from which the compiler derives every role, by `anchor-v1` or `anchor-v2`;
- a `generator`, for #20's generated schemes.

A scheme with one of the two base modes is single-mode, and pins it; a stable scheme has both. `high-contrast` sets derive from the base ones (`high-contrast-v2` for an `anchor-v2` scheme, `high-contrast-v1` otherwise) unless the scheme authors `highContrast.light` and `highContrast.dark`. `swatches`, up to eight colors, is what a picker shows.

A generated scheme names a `generator`, its version, and the input it reads, and has no colors of its own. Four ship (#20):

- `brand-seed` (Extended), from a site's `appearanceInputs` seed color: the reviewed canvas, text, and secondary in each mode, with the seed as the accent.
- `hct-tonal` (Extended), from a seed color: HCT, computed first-party from the published CAM16 (Li et al., 2017) and CIE L* formulas, as Material Design defines the color space, with every anchor a tone of the seed's palettes. Primary is the seed's hue at 48 chroma or more, tertiary is 60 degrees on at 24, and neutral is at 4. Light takes neutral 99 and 10 and primary and tertiary 40, and dark takes neutral 10 and 90 and primary and tertiary 80. It's credited, not affiliated with Google, and it doesn't claim Material's own tones: from `#6750a4` it lands within one 8-bit step of M3's published baseline in every channel, not on it.
- `profile-image` and `portfolio-artwork` (Experimental), from a media asset: `image-palette@1`, this project's own quantizer. It samples a 64 by 64 grid of the decoded pixels, clusters a 4-bit-per-channel histogram within 0.08 in OKLab, and scores each cluster by its share times its OKLCH chroma. The top color is the accent, and the next one at least 30 degrees of hue away is the secondary, or the reviewed one when nothing is, over the reviewed canvas and text.

Each generator then holds the accent's OKLCH lightness from 0.15 to 0.9, so its hover and active states have room, and anchor-v2 and high-contrast-v2 solve the rest. The refined body's step for `on-accent` needs no code, since black or white reads at 4.5:1 on every color. A generator is pure and browser-safe (`@portfolio/appearance/generators`). Its result is the record a draft save stores:

- the generator and version, and the algorithm;
- the input: a seed color, or an asset id with the SHA-256 of its decoded pixels;
- the colors an image scored;
- the anchors as picked and as used;
- every move, the generator's and anchor-v2's;
- all four sets;
- a contrast report at the strictest readings any shipping pack gives: textured and opaque, glassmorphism's 97% over canvas, black, and white, and opaque under each way a shipping language washes its surfaces (Material 3's 4% accent tint, and Fluent's 28% canvas sheen beside its grain, raised).

A draft save stores a scheme only when every pair on a bare surface passes, and every pair under a wash that a language the site renders with lays. Those languages are its `designLanguage` (its survivor when the id is retired, the fallback when the build has none by it), and every language visitors are offered when they may choose; a language whose texture the gate can't read counts as laying every shipping wash. A site on platform-neutral alone is never refused for Material 3's tint, so a stored scheme's own report may fail on a wash pair no language of its site lays. A refusal names the wash and the pair, such as `Under material-3-inspired's 4% accent wash, text-muted on surface-prominent in dark is 4.41:1, under 4.5:1`, and when the language is one visitors may choose rather than the site's own, it says to lock the design language. Three paths render a stored scheme under a language the save didn't read, and stay ungated: an owner preview or Studio swap or compare that overrides the design language, the demo profile, where every axis is selectable, and an import that keeps an accepted image-derived entry from the live revision. None reaches a visitor with a shipping generator today, since the reference records and a sweep of 3,072 seeds pass every wash.

A page renders a generated scheme only from what was stored for it, a draft's `derived.schemes` in a preview and the live revision's `snapshots/schemes.json` on a visitor page. It checks each entry when it reads it, and never runs a generator. An entry that's missing, malformed, or made by another generator or version falls back on its axis.

Each generated scheme has a reference record in `packages/appearance-schemes/generated/<id>.json`, which `pnpm catalog:generated` writes from its reference input. The seeds are `#5b5bd6` and `#6750a4`, and the images are `example-media/anamika/portrait.png` and `example-media/gallery/looking-1.png`, the repository's own drawings with no metadata. A record written before K-2 stays current while it matches in every other role and in its contrast report, less the pairs that read the three roles K-2 added: its sets get the three when they render, as a stored draft's do, so the owner's acceptance of it stands. A record written at fewer of the strictest readings, as all four are (their `reading` names the first two), stays current while every other field matches and its report is what those readings make of the same sets: the washed readings changed what the report reads, not a color, so the generators stay at version 1 and every stored draft and revision that pins it renders as before. The same holds for the packs' focus targets: version 1 keeps solving `focus` and `focus-inverse` to 3:1 and 3.5:1 (below). A draft stores a generated scheme without them too, so a scheme a revision froze before K-2 and the same scheme saved now are one entry, and raise no change.

`pnpm check:appearance` runs each generator again. It fails when a record is stale, and when the bytes change at the same generator version, which has to go up first. It gates each registered generated scheme's reference sets with every language and profile, as it gates an authored scheme.

An image-derived scheme stays out of every build until the owner accepts it. Registry generation leaves it out, as it does an excluded pack, unless the package's `generated/acceptance.json` pins the SHA-256 of its current reference record. Selecting it until then renders the axis's fallback with `appearance.unavailable`, which blocks publish, and a new record makes the acceptance stale. Before accepting, the owner reads `pnpm catalog show <id>` and the page `pnpm catalog review` writes, which puts the source image beside every role in all four sets. Then `pnpm catalog accept <id> --by <login>` writes the acceptance, and the owner commits it. A site's own image-derived output waits for the owner's approval before it publishes, as #113's derived changes do.

`anchor-v1` (#19) mixes every role from the anchors and moves a derived role only when it fails its gate pair: 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for non-text, 7:1 and 4.5:1 in high contrast. Every shipping scheme uses `anchor-v2` (#20), which starts from anchor-v1's set and re-solves the roles that read on the canvas and surfaces to the targets a textured design language sets, 1.1 times those: 4.95:1 for `text-secondary`, `text-muted`, and `accent-text`, 3.3:1 for `border-strong`, the categorical colors, and the large-text signature fill, and 7.7:1 and 4.95:1 in high contrast. So any language whose texture is marks only passes with any scheme; a wash takes contrast of its own, which the gate composites and holds each language to (below). It keeps a scheme's character: the anchors never move, nor do the fills, the accent states, or the statuses, and a role moves only when it fails, by the least step past its target plus 0.1. The text roles, `focus`, and `border-strong` step toward text in 2%. The signature fill moves the fewest of its two ends, then the least distance, toward text. The categorical walk resolves the six categorical colors in order, from the accent, secondary, the statuses, and `text-secondary`, each taking the smallest 2% OKLCH lightness step that passes on `surface-default` and keeps it 0.015 in OKLab from the ones before it. `adjustments` records every role anchor-v2 moved from anchor-v1, by set, as `[anchor-v1's value, anchor-v2's]`, and validation holds the record to what the solver gives, so it can't drift.

`focus` and `focus-inverse` are held above their gate pairs (lead A's ruling, 11-01). In a scheme pack's base sets both derivations solve `focus` to 4.5:1 on its worst ground, the canvas or any surface, by the same 2% steps toward text from the accent, and `focus-inverse` to 4.5:1 on `text-primary`, by 2% steps toward the canvas (`FOCUS_TARGETS`): a ring reads like body text wherever it lands. An authored set gives a `focus` that reaches it too: poster-primaries' light ring is `#2646e0`, its blue a step deeper so it reads at 4.67:1 on the poster's yellow `surface-prominent`. Since anchor-v1 already reaches 4.5:1, anchor-v2 never moves `focus` and `adjustments` doesn't list it. A high-contrast set is unchanged: its `focus` is the text color and its `focus-inverse` was already at 4.5:1. The move shifted 32 base sets' `focus` and 55 base sets' `focus-inverse` (in every case but two past the derived-role bound of 2.0 ΔE00 or 5 degrees of hue), so the lead reviewed each before it shipped, from a before-and-after report of every set's colors, ratios, ΔE00, and hue shift. That report covers the scheme packs only, not generated schemes, which don't move: #20's generators stay at version 1, and version 1 derives to the targets it shipped with, `focus` at 3:1 (3.3:1 once anchor-v2 raises it) and `focus-inverse` at 3.5:1 (`GENERATOR_V1_FOCUS_TARGETS`), when it generates and when a stored scheme renders, so every reference record, stored draft, and revision keeps its bytes. Those still pass the gate's floors. A version 2 at the packs' targets, with a migration for the schemes stored at version 1, is a later item.

Two optional anchors per mode extend anchor-v2, and only anchor-v2 (S1):

- `emphasis`, one hex: the `emphasis` role takes it as given, in place of the accent, in the base set. Since K-2 the gate reads `emphasis` as a fill: `on-emphasis` on it at 4.5:1, and 7:1 in a high-contrast set. That's a tightening: an authored high-contrast set with a mid-tone `emphasis`, such as `#777777`, where the better of black and white is 4.69:1, passed before and fails now. Otherwise it's a decorative third hue, never text but `on-emphasis`, or the only marker of anything. A high-contrast set keeps the solved accent.
- `categorical`, one to six hex: the categorical walk's first starts. In the base set they take the places of the accent, secondary, and statuses, in that order, and `text-secondary` stays the last start, so five give a family of five and six replace the walk's starts outright. The high-contrast sets walk the same anchors in place of their own first starts (the accent, `information`, and the statuses). A single-mode scheme's high-contrast set of the other polarity walks the one mode's anchors, as it derives from that mode's set. The walk holds each to its target and 0.015 apart, as always.

`adjustments` records their moves too. A scheme with neither derives exactly what it did before: a golden test holds the 25 anchor-v2 schemes that shipped before S1 to byte-identical sets, in every role but the three K-2 added (`packages/appearance/src/__fixtures__/anchor-v2-sets.sha256.json`), and `derived-roles.sha256.json` beside it pins those three.

### A design language

`tokens` gives every `--ui-*` key a value, each held to its key's grammar and caps, and reaching color only through `var(--color-*)`. A rule always draws: compositions use `border-divider` for structure, so its width is at least 1px or 0.0625rem, its style is never `none`, and its color is never `transparent`. A language restyles a rule, never removes it. An optional `stylesheet`, such as `./styles.css`, styles the primitives' `data-ui-slot` parts and their states, and nothing else: the reader takes a small subset of CSS, sets only the properties a language owns, takes colors only as `var(--color-*)`, keeps lengths within 64px or 4rem and radii within 999px, and refuses `@import`, `@font-face`, `:root`, `html`, `body`, `!important`, color literals, and generated content. The compiler writes the rules back out inside `@scope ([data-design-language="<id>"]) to ([data-appearance-boundary])`, from what it read, so nothing in the file can reach outside its boundary.

The stylesheet is untrusted input, so the reader parses it, and the pack CSS lint names the rule each refusal breaks: `url` (no `url()`, `src()`, `image-set()`, `image()`, `cross-fade()`, or `element()`, since a language declares no asset), `import`, `layer-scope` (no `@layer` or `@scope` of its own), `universal` (no `*`), `prefixed-names` (`@keyframes`, `@counter-style`, and `view-transition-name` start with the pack's id, though the reader refuses all three anyway), `focus-outline` (nothing removes an outline, and a focus rule draws no `box-shadow` ring), `javascript` (no `expression()`, `javascript:` URL, `behavior`, or `-moz-binding`), and `on-fill` (a part the shared primitives draw in `on-*` text, such as a status badge, a primary action, or a field's error, takes a `background` or `background-color` only of a fill the contrast gate holds that text to, such as `var(--color-danger)` under `on-status`, and an inset `box-shadow` only in such a fill, since it paints over the fill, under the text, and a language never colors text and can't move the text to match its fill; not yet linted: a fill on a child part that inherits `on-*` text, such as an action's label inside a primary action, `background-image`, and an inset shadow a `--ui-shadow-*` token carries in the manifest, such as the raised shadow the primitives give every toast). A rule holds in any case, behind a comment, and behind a `\` escape: the reader refuses every escape, then reads the stylesheet as a browser decodes it and names any rule the escape hid. Each rule has a known-bad input, `packages/appearance/test/fixtures/lint-<rule>/`, that `pnpm check:appearance` requires to fail for that rule.

### A typography profile

`roles` gives each of the ten roles all six fields: a font stack ending in a generic family, a rem size up to 16rem, a weight, a unitless line height, a letter spacing in em, and variation settings. `measures` gives `body` and `compact` as counts of characters, written in `ch`, such as `68ch` and `52ch`. They mean characters, not `ch` units: a `ch` is the width of the digit 0, about 1.2 average letters in these faces, so `68ch` set 84 to 94 characters a line. The compiler resolves each to that many of the body face's average advance, plus the body's letter-spacing, which every character takes, in em (lead A's ruling R11). The advance is the body's own shipped face, read from its file at the body's weight and size, else the first face in the body stack that `TEXT_FACES` in `font-metrics.ts` has: every `SYSTEM_FONTS` face and the system faces OS-stack packs lead with (Trebuchet MS, Charter, Iowan Old Style, and SF Pro for `system-ui`), each measured once from this Mac's files as 16px body text renders it, so SF Pro at weight 400 and optical size 17, its Text design, not its Display default. Those are the macOS faces: on another platform a stack's first face may be another, such as Segoe UI for `system-ui` on Windows, whose width the measure doesn't follow. So `humanist-ui`'s `68ch` compiles to `--type-measure-body: calc(31.21em * clamp(0.5, var(--layout-measure-scale, 1), 2))`, at Trebuchet MS's 0.4589em, with a comment naming the face and its advance. The count is of characters as they render (lead A's ruling R11.1): under `font-size-adjust`, the body face renders at the aspect it's adjusted to over its own, so Inter, whose x-height is 0.5459 of its size, renders at 0.916 of it under the platform's 0.5, and 68 counted characters would hold about 74. So the profile also sets `--type-body-aspect`, the face's x-height per em, read from the same file or table (OS/2's `sxHeight`, or the top of the `x` glyph for a font whose OS/2 has none, as Trebuchet MS, Charter, and Iowan Old Style do), and a composition that applies `font-size-adjust` sets `--layout-measure-scale` from it. The scale is set on the boundary, in the composition's root rule, because the measures are custom properties declared there, and a `var()` in one resolves where it's declared, not where it's read. A face whose x-height isn't known gets no `--type-body-aspect`, and the scale stays 1. The measure holds the scale from 0.5 to 2 (`clamp()`), wider than any aspect over any face gives, so a composition's scale of 0, or a typo, can't collapse a column or blow it out. A body stack with neither keeps its declared `ch`, about 1.2 characters each, with a warning that registry generation prints, so a pack that compiled before still does. `measures.test.ts` holds every shipping pack's compiled body measure to 68 characters of its face, and compact to 52, within 2, both at its font-size and under `font-size-adjust` at the scale a composition sets, with each advance and aspect measured apart from the compiler's table: the shipped file, or the system face's own file where it's installed, or an advance the test records. The kit's running text, captions, subtitles, and taglines read these tokens, never a width in `ch`. `scripts` lists the ISO 15924 codes every role covers. `fontAssets`, up to 32, are the `.woff2` files it ships, each with its `family`, `weight` (or a range), `style`, and the `unicodeRange` it covers, so a browser downloads it only for text that uses it. Where a family's subsets at one weight and style overlap, as Fontsource's latin and latin-ext do at U+0304, U+0308, U+0329, and U+2020 (†), the compiler writes the Latin face last and keeps each shared code point on one face, the one whose file's `cmap` maps it: the Latin face when both do (U+0304 and U+0308, so they load nothing more), else the subset that has the glyph (U+2020: Fontsource's Latin files have no †, so a † costs the latin-ext file, which draws it), else neither (U+0329, which no file maps, falls through to the stack). Chromium loads every face whose range meets the text, so a code point loads one file at most, and the one that draws it. A subset left with nothing to draw gets no face. The compiler namespaces each family as `"<pack id> <family>"` and writes `font-display: swap`.

### Shipping fonts

A typography profile that ships fonts ships OFL (or Apache-2.0 or MIT) files, vendored into its pack directory and committed with it. Nothing fetches a font at build or run time, and the host serves each from `/_portfolio/font/` under the `font-src 'self'` the policy already has.

**Packages.** Each family comes from an exact-pinned `@fontsource/<family>` or `@fontsource-variable/<family>` package. Those ship one pre-subset WOFF2 per unicode range (latin, latin-ext, cyrillic, greek, vietnamese, and so on), so no subsetting tool is needed. The lead adds each package, one commit at a time, so the lockfile carries its integrity. Use `@fontsource-variable` when a composition uses two or more weights of a family and its variable latin file is no bigger than those static files combined; otherwise use the statics. Record the size comparison with the pack.

**Vendoring.** `pnpm catalog:fonts vendor <pack> <package> <files...>` copies the named files, such as `files/inter-latin-400-normal.woff2`, and the package's `LICENSE` from `node_modules` into the pack: the pack's own package's `node_modules` first, then the root's. The license lands as `LICENSE-<package>.txt`, such as `LICENSE-fontsource-inter.txt`. It never fetches: a package that isn't installed is refused, and so are a pack id or package name that isn't one and a package whose `package.json` names another. Vendor only the subsets the composition's content can need. It prints what the package's own stylesheet says of each file (family, weight, style, `unicode-range`) for the manifest's `fontAssets`, and you add those entries and the manifest's `cost` by hand.

**The fit rule.** A file named with a trailing `?` is optional. One whose range meets printable Basic Latin is added only while the pack's Latin faces stay at or under 102,400 B; any other, such as a latin-ext subset, is always added, since a Latin page never loads it (review R4). The Latin faces are what a Latin page loads and what the pack gate counts, while the rest are reported. Optional Latin files are taken by package and path, whatever order or call they're vendored in. When a required file leaves an optional one the pack already ships no room, vendoring refuses it rather than keep a pack that depends on the vendoring order. Each entry records `latin` and, when vendored optional, `optional`. What the rule leaves out goes into the record's `dropped` list, by package, version, path, and bytes. Vendoring warns when the required Latin faces alone are over the cap, which `pnpm check:appearance` then fails. `--into fonts` puts the files and the license in the pack's `fonts/` directory, under their names lowercased.

**Provenance.** Vendoring writes `fonts.provenance.json` beside the manifest, one entry per WOFF2 file:

```json
{
  "version": 1,
  "fonts": [
    {
      "file": "inter-latin-400-normal.woff2",
      "sha256": "<64 hex digits>",
      "source": {
        "package": "@fontsource/inter",
        "version": "5.2.6",
        "path": "files/inter-latin-400-normal.woff2"
      },
      "license": { "spdx": "OFL-1.1", "file": "LICENSE-fontsource-inter.txt" }
    }
  ]
}
```

`pnpm check:appearance` and `pnpm catalog:fonts verify` hold every typography pack to it, fixture packs included: every WOFF2 in the pack is listed, every listed file is there with the bytes its `sha256` names, its license is OFL-1.1, Apache-2.0, or MIT, and the license file it names is in the pack. A fixture package's pack may say `"source": "fixture"`; no other may. Never edit a vendored font; vendor it again.

**Fallbacks.** For each family a role renders in, the compiler reads the font's own `head`, `hhea`, `OS/2`, `cmap`, and `hmtx` tables (`packages/appearance/src/node/font-metrics.ts`, which decompresses WOFF2's Brotli stream with Node's `zlib`) and writes up to three fallback aliases over local system faces (packs-plan §3.4).

- **Width.** A face's width is measured from the glyphs, the same way for the webfont and every system face: the advance of a to z and the space, each weighted by how often it occurs in English text, read through `cmap` and `hmtx` (a WOFF2 transformed `hmtx` keeps its advances, so it reads as well). OS/2's `xAvgCharWidth` isn't used: fonts store different averages there. The x-height is OS/2's `sxHeight`.
- **The table.** `SYSTEM_FONTS` in `font-metrics.ts` holds ten system faces by class, each measured once from this Mac's own files, with the method, file, units per em, width, and x-height in its comment, and its bold, italic, and bold italic measured the same way: Helvetica Neue, Arial, Avenir Next, Tahoma, and Verdana (sans); Georgia, Palatino, and Times New Roman (serif); Menlo and Courier New (mono). Avenir Next also has its Demi Bold, the one semibold, which stands under upright text at 600 to 699, so a 600 heading in Space Grotesk doesn't fall back to a bolder 700; every other face takes its bold there. Helvetica Neue has its Medium, the one medium, which stands under upright text at 500 to 599, so Inter at 500 doesn't fall back to the lighter Regular; every other face takes its regular there. A test measures each file and variant again wherever it's installed. Every macOS name in it resolves through `local()` on a stock Mac. SF Mono isn't in it: macOS keeps it as a hidden system face that `local()` can't reach.
- **Best fit.** The role stack's generic family gives the class, and the aliases are the class's faces that fit the family best by width per x-height: the face's width over its x-height against the font's, less 1. That's the error a composition is left with once it applies `font-size-adjust`, which scales every face to one x-height, whatever role aspect it sets (0.5 by default, 0.45 to 0.56 per pack), so line breaks depend on it and not on `size-adjust`. A font with no x-height is fitted by plain width. Each alias's CSS comment gives both errors: per x-height, and its plain width error before `size-adjust`, which `size-adjust` corrects where no `font-size-adjust` applies. The aliases are picked at weight 400 and the body's optical size, where most text renders. The picks for the variants-10 families, which tests hold against the table, come from the plan's metrics and from the installed packages' own latin files:
  - Helvetica Neue for Inter;
  - Tahoma for Archivo, whose variable file defaults to weight 600 but at 400 is the static face the plan measured;
  - Avenir Next for Space Grotesk;
  - Palatino for Newsreader and Fraunces;
  - Menlo for JetBrains Mono and IBM Plex Mono.

  Each style a family ships is fitted on its own, the italic against each face's italic, by the italic file's metrics. Newsreader's italic, which `swiss-grotesque` ships alone, gets Georgia Italic. A family that ships both styles gets an italic fit of its own too: Fraunces' 600 upright gets Palatino Bold, and a 400 italic beside it gets Times New Roman Italic rather than Palatino Italic, which runs 11% narrow per x-height against it. (`field-serif` names that italic "Fraunces Italic", a family of its own, so its captions and quotes are always italic.)

- **Faces at each weight and style.** Each alias has a face for each weight the family's roles set, and for 400 and 700 (text inside a role), in each style the family ships, wherever the family has a face covering that weight. At 600 or more it stands on the system face's bold, and for an italic family on its italic (`systemVariant()`), by that face's own `local()` names. Each is sized to the webfont as that text renders it:
  - a variable font at the weight, read through its `fvar`, `avar`, and `HVAR` tables (`readFontMetrics(bytes, name, { wght, opsz })`);
  - with an optical-size axis, at the largest role size set at that weight too, as `font-optical-sizing: auto` sets it.

  Where the family has no face at a weight, there's no face for it, and the browser synthesizes the bold from its nearest face for the webfont and the fallback alike. A heading at 800 then swaps from Helvetica Neue Bold sized to Archivo at 800, not from a regular face drawn at body weight.

- **No faked bold in an italic-only family.** A family that ships only italics, as `field-serif`'s Fraunces Italic and `swiss-grotesque`'s Newsreader do, has its faces and its fallback aliases declared from their own weight to 900, `font-weight: 400 900`, so a bold in its roles matches the real 400 italic, with the web font and with its fallback alike, and the browser fakes no weight. Only that family: every other face keeps its own weight, so a bold over a family with no bold face, such as Plex Mono 500 in `swiss-grotesque`, `paste-up`, and `drafting-grotesk`, is still synthesized, and a composition's bold caption title or current-chapter label keeps its weight cue.
- **Two aliases.** `"<pack id> <family> Fallback 1"` is the best fit, by its macOS names. `"<pack id> <family> Fallback 2"` is the best fit among the faces Windows and Linux have, by the names they go by there: the face itself where Windows ships it, then its metric clones, such as Liberation Sans and Arimo for Arial, DejaVu Sans Mono for Menlo, Palatino Linotype and TeX Gyre Pagella for Palatino, Liberation Serif and Tinos for Times New Roman, and Liberation Mono and Cousine for Courier New. Each `src` is a `local()` list of up to four names, full and PostScript, and the second leaves out any name the first lists; a family whose first alias already covers Windows and Linux takes the next fit for its second. Inter gets Helvetica Neue, then Arial or its clones.
- **A Linux face.** A chain whose two aliases have no face a stock Linux has gets a third, `"<pack id> <family> Fallback 3"`, over the class's Liberation face (`LIBERATION_FONTS` in `font-metrics.ts`: Liberation Sans, Serif, and Mono, with Arial's, Times New Roman's, and Courier New's metrics), by its Linux names and its Croscore clone's (Arimo, Tinos, Cousine). Ubuntu's `fonts-liberation` installs them, on CI's Playwright image and a stock desktop alike, where DejaVu may not be; macOS, Windows, and iOS install none of those names, so the third is never reached there. Without it, Linux rendered those families in an unmatched face until the webfont loaded, and the swap reflowed the page: the likely cause of the 13 px Swiss Signal's hold moved on CI, where swiss-grotesque's Newsreader italic and IBM Plex Mono had no face to stand on. Before it, the chains with no Linux face were book-serif's Fraunces and Newsreader (Palatino, then Palatino Linotype and TeX Gyre Pagella; Georgia Italic, then Gelasio Italic), drafting-grotesk's and paste-up's Space Grotesk (Avenir Next, then Verdana), field-serif's Fraunces at 600, and swiss-grotesque's Newsreader italic; every mono family's chain over Menlo also gets Liberation Mono, since DejaVu Sans Mono may be missing. A test fails for any pack whose chain, at any weight and style, has no Liberation or Croscore name.
- **Overrides.** Each alias's `size-adjust` makes its face's average Latin character as wide as the font's, and its `ascent-override`, `descent-override`, and `line-gap-override` give the font's own vertical metrics at that size (the typo metrics when the font sets USE_TYPO_METRICS, `hhea`'s otherwise), so the swap moves no line.
- **Coverage.** The aliases are picked by each style's Latin face nearest weight 400; a style with no face that maps Latin lowercase gets no fallback rather than a guessed one. The aliases' `unicode-range` is their family's, so text outside it falls through to the stack's next family.

The stack becomes `"<pack id> <family>", "<pack id> <family> Fallback 1", "<pack id> <family> Fallback 2", <declared stack>`, with `"... Fallback 3"` after the second where a family has one. A file whose tables don't decode fails the compile, naming the file, and so does a family named like another's fallback alias, such as `Inter Fallback 1` (or 2, or 3).

**Linux fallbacks for system stacks.** A system-stack pack ships no font, and Linux installs neither Trebuchet MS nor Verdana, so on Ubuntu, CI's Playwright image among them, `humanist-ui` and `accessible-reading` render in Liberation Sans, Arial's metric clone, or in DejaVu Sans, and their text takes other room than on a Mac. So a role whose stack leads with a family in `LEADING_FAMILIES` (`font-metrics.ts`: Trebuchet MS and Verdana, each face measured once from this Mac's files, with its `hhea` metrics and the Latin page it draws) gets two metric fallbacks right behind that family: `"<pack id> <family> Linux Fallback"`, over `local("Liberation Sans")`, Arimo, then Arial, and `"<pack id> <family> DejaVu Fallback"`, over DejaVu Sans, its own face since its widths aren't Arial's. Each has a face at 400 and 700, upright and italic, the four the family has, with `size-adjust` and the overrides computed as above against the family's own face, so Trebuchet MS's 400 becomes Liberation Sans at 102.67% and Verdana's 700 Liberation Sans Bold at 118.78%. Their `unicode-range` is what the family draws on a Latin page (Verdana has no U+2010), so where it's installed, as on macOS, Windows, and iOS, every code point they could draw is drawn by it first and they're never used. Only the first entry counts: a family further down a stack is reached only where the one before it is missing anyway. `humanist-ui`'s body stack becomes `"Trebuchet MS", "humanist-ui Trebuchet MS Linux Fallback", "humanist-ui Trebuchet MS DejaVu Fallback", "Segoe UI", sans-serif`; `geometric-display`'s Trebuchet MS roles get the same.

- **Residual: DejaVu Sans.** It's what a desktop Linux without an Arial clone renders, and it isn't installed on macOS. Its widths were measured from the DejaVu Sans 2.35 files matplotlib bundles, not Ubuntu's 2.37, and its fallback was never rendered here: its `size-adjust` (89.43% under Trebuchet MS at 400, 82.66% at 700, about 100% under Verdana) matches the weighted average width only, and its own glyph proportions differ more from Trebuchet MS's than Liberation Sans' do. A test measures the Linux files wherever they're installed, within 0.2%.
- **Not covered.** The other system families a stack leads with (Avenir Next, Iowan Old Style, Futura, `system-ui`) are missing on Linux too, and keep the declared stack there.

**Preload.** A page preloads one font at most: its typography's body face, upright, at the body role's weight, in the subset that covers Latin, as `<link rel="preload" as="font" type="font/woff2" crossorigin>` from the document boundary. A plate preloads nothing, a system-font profile nothing, and display faces load when text needs them. The trace's purity check fails a page with more than one font preload, or one of a unit it doesn't render.

**Budgets.** A typography's `font` budget, at most 100 KB (`runtime-budgets.json`), holds what a page loads under it, not what it ships. `pnpm check:budgets` gates its sample page line: an estimate of the bytes of the faces a page loads, by the `unicode-range` of a traced page's text when the trace rendered a page under it, otherwise its Latin faces: every face whose `unicode-range` meets a Latin page's text anywhere, so a caps-only display face, or one of curly quotes alone, counts too. A Latin page's text is `LATIN_PAGE` in `font-metrics.ts`: printable Basic Latin (U+0020 to U+007E), Latin-1's letters and signs (U+00A0 to U+00FF), the hyphen, dashes, curly quotes, bullet, and ellipsis, the euro sign, and the trade mark sign. Google's latin-ext range doesn't meet it, so a latin-ext face isn't counted. The bytes of every file it ships are reported, as an `info` line, and never fail, since a page loads latin-ext, cyrillic, or another subset only when its text needs one. `pnpm check:appearance`'s pack gate holds its Latin faces to the same budget, and its `cost` to every file. The compiler holds each face's declared `unicode-range` to its file: a range that claims a Basic Latin code point the font's `cmap` doesn't map fails the compile, so the ranges these gates read say what the files hold. When the package's own range is the one that claims too much, as an older font missing a few ASCII glyphs might, narrow the range to the code points the file maps. Still vendor only the subsets the composition's content can need, and keep a page's faces small: a display-heavy profile declares a variable range rather than many static weights.

## Building, checking, and installing

Install a pack the way you install a variant: `pnpm --filter @portfolio/web add <package or tarball>`. Registry generation, which every build runs, validates each pack's manifest, compiles it into one stylesheet in its layer, writes that file by content hash to `/_portfolio/pack/` and each font to `/_portfolio/font/`, and lists them in the registry the host reads. The host links only the selected packs' stylesheets, with the platform's, on each page. A pack that doesn't validate or compile stops the build with a diagnostic naming the package, the file, the pointer, and the fix, in the same codes a variant's manifest uses.

`pnpm check:appearance` then gates what registered: the computed contrast of every design language, color scheme, typography, and display mode combination against the targets in `quality/thresholds.json`, each scheme's hierarchy (its accent states and quieter text roles stay apart), and the layer order of every layered stylesheet. The focus ring is held to 3:1 (4.5:1 in high contrast) on the canvas and every surface around it; a scheme pack derives it to 4.5:1 there, above the gate's floor. A flush ring, a `focus-offset` of 0 or less, touches the part it rings, so it is also held to that target against each fill a focusable primitive paints at its edge (`FOCUS_FILLS`: `accent` and its hover and active states, for the primary Action, the skip link, a selected chip's border, and a checked box; `surface-default`, `surface-subtle`, and `surface-prominent`, for the secondary and quiet Actions, Field and Select, and the selected Tab), as `focus-inside` pairs. A ring offset outward isn't skipped: the ground shows between it and the fill, so the focus-on-ground pairs already measure what it reads against. A language's `texture` is read as washes and marks, failing closed (`textureLayers()`). A mark is a `repeating-*` gradient, or one where every stop that lays a role ends its band, at its own position or the next stop's, in px or rem within 24px (`MARK_MAX_PX`): a grain dot, a 1px grid line, a stripe, a hatch, a corner shape. It covers too little to composite, so a language with any mark has its targets on the canvas and surfaces raised by 10% as a budget for it (`textureRaise`). Every other layer is a wash, in any direction, shape, or color space: a role laid across a surface (Material 3's tonal tint), from an edge (Fluent's acrylic sheen), in a band, or as a vignette. The gate composites each role a wash lays, at its strongest stop, over each surface, never the canvas and never in a high-contrast set (which drops the texture), and holds every pair there to its target; a failure names the wash (`under the 4% accent wash`) and is the language's, unless the bare pair fails too. A texture with both gets both. A stop the gate can't read (anything but `transparent`, `var(--color-<role>)`, or a role mixed with `transparent`) fails the gate. The raise once covered washes too, and passed Material 3's first tint, 8% of the accent, with 71 text pairs under 4.5:1; `packages/appearance/test/fixtures/bad-texture-wash/` holds that tint as a known-bad input. A raised object's or a card's shadow falls on the ground beside it, where a caption sits, so the gate reads the key layer of `shadow-raised` and `shadow-card` (`shadowKey()`: of the outer layers with a blur, the one that reaches furthest below its object; an inset layer, or one with no blur, casts nothing there), composites `--color-shadow` at half its alpha over the canvas and each surface (`SHADOW_KEY_PEAK`: a Gaussian blur lays half its alpha at the edge of its own box, where content beside the object starts), and holds `text-secondary`, at the label role's size, to 4.5:1 there in every set. A failure names the key layer (`beside the shadow-raised key layer at 36%`) and is the language's, unless the bare pair fails too, and a shadow layer the gate can't read fails the gate. `packages/appearance/test/fixtures/bad-shadow-key/` holds fluent-2-inspired's old raised shadow, a 36% key at `0 32px 64px`, which CI run 41510373684 found OA's captions at 4.00:1 beside. Six languages failed the check the day it landed, and `SHADOW_KEY_KNOWN_FAILURES` lists each with its worst pair (synthwave 3.49:1, fluent-2-inspired 3.90:1 (4.00:1 on warm-parchment, where CI found it), skeuomorphic 4.02:1, carbon-inspired 4.21:1, atelier-luxury 4.30:1, glassmorphism 4.33:1, each on botanical-sage's light `surface-prominent`): known failures, each with a ticket, not a waiver. A listed language's shadow-key pairs at or above its ratio don't fail the run, and one under it does; a listed language that passes now, whose worst has risen, or that isn't registered fails the run until its entry comes off or rises, so the list only shrinks. CI runs it on every pull request, and a combination under its targets fails the run. A failure excludes one pack, never a pair: a focus ring's width, a flush ring on a fill, and a translucent surface's backdrop are the design language's, and every other pair the color scheme's.

It also runs the per-axis pack gates over every compiled pack: token coverage (every block that sets an axis's tokens sets all of the contract's roles), bundle size (the stylesheet within the pack's `css` budget in gzip bytes, a typography's Latin faces, each whose range meets a Latin page's text, within `font`, and `cost` what ships), reduced motion (a design language's `motion-fast` within `quality/thresholds.json`'s `reducedMotion.transitionMs`, since reduced motion runs every duration at it), and boundary isolation (one layer, the axis's; every rule on the pack's own boundary attribute; a language's own rules in its one `@scope`; and every `url()` one of its own fonts). It checks the catalog lock, `packages/appearance/appearance-catalog.lock.json`, which pins each first-party pack's version, tier, license, and content digest: a pack whose files changed without a version bump fails, and so does a lock that is stale or out of its canonical form. In CI, `CATALOG_LOCK_BASE=HEAD^1` holds it to the lock at the change's base too, so a pack changed with its lock edited to match, at the version the base pins, still fails, and so does a version below the base's. `pnpm catalog:lock` writes it, with `apps/web/public/third-party-notices.txt`, which has to be current too: a notice for every pack a production registry registers under an upstream license, an installed third party's too, with its source, attribution, and copyright line, the MIT permission notice under an MIT pack's, and the full Apache-2.0 or OFL-1.1 text once after the notices. The lock pins first-party packs alone, since `pnpm-lock.yaml` pins an installed one. And it checks every Research record's shape and that its id is no pack's or variant's. Last, the branded-name audit (`apps/web/scripts/catalog-audit.ts`) reads every shipping pack and Research record for the brands, systems, palettes, and typefaces it names: a pack names one in its id or name only as an `-inspired` interpretation or under that brand's own upstream license, credits any it names in its description, never shares two anchor colors in a mode with a Research record, or with an upstream palette it doesn't credit under that palette's license and copyright line (`apps/web/scripts/upstream-palettes.json`, Tailwind CSS v3.4.17's scales today), and, for a typography profile, is never named for a typeface its stacks reference. In CI each finding is a failure record naming its pack and axis, and its token role, display mode, and tuple where it has them.

`pnpm catalog` reads the same registry and prints the catalog: `pnpm catalog search 'q=sage&axis=scheme&tier=stable'` lists what a query matches, by axis and tier, with filters `q`, `axis` (`language`, `scheme`, or `type`), `tier`, `status` (`shipping`, `excluded`, `held`, or `research`), `license`, and `mode`; `pnpm catalog show <id>` gives one entry's tier, provenance, and whether this build can select it, or why not; `pnpm catalog recipe [<id>]` applies a recipe from `packages/appearance/appearance-recipes.json` as the five ordinary axis ids it stands for, with their query and a diagnostic for each id this build doesn't register; and `pnpm catalog audit` prints the branded-name audit. The shipping packs, the Research records, and the recipes were seeded once from the reviewed prototype's catalog, copied byte for byte to `scripts/catalog/seed/`, by `pnpm catalog:seed`, which never writes over a file. The manifests are canonical now.

A translucent design language ships at its translucency floor: the least `surface-opacity`, in whole percent from the approved value up, at which every surface passes over the worst of the canvas, black, and white, with every shipping scheme and profile. The manifest keeps the approved value in `approvedSurfaceOpacity`, and the lock records both, as `surfaceOpacity.approved` and `surfaceOpacity.shipped`. A language whose floor is 100% is opaque in effect: it ships opaque only on a recorded decision, keeping the approved value in `approvedSurfaceOpacity` and adding a caveat that it ships opaque (#20 PR 2d, eight languages). Otherwise it stays held for the owner's call.

A pack that fails its gate with no fix in scope is held: its manifest stays as seeded in its package's `held/<id>/manifest.json`, which registry generation never reads, beside a `gate.json` that `pnpm catalog:held` writes from the gate, with the failing checks, every failing role pair at its worst with its target and scheme, and, for a translucent language, the least surface opacity that passes. `pnpm check:appearance` prints each held pack with its reason, and fails when one is registered, when its record is stale, or when it passes now, so it can be promoted. The production build check holds a held id to what it holds a Research id to, and `pnpm catalog search 'status=held'` lists them.

A pack that duplicates another is retired, on any axis. Its directory moves unchanged, with `git mv`, from `packs/` to its package's `retired/<id>/`, beside a `retired.json` that keeps its id as an alias of the pack that replaced it, so no stored or linked selection breaks.

- **The record:** `retiredVersion` (1), `id` (its directory), `kind` (its package's), `aliasOf` (the survivor's id), `reason`, `change`, `survivorVersion`, `survivorDigest`, and `retiredAt` (YYYY-MM-DD). `change` is a clause saying what looked different under the survivor at the merge, such as "at the merge, its icon strokes became slightly thinner", or null when it looked the same. `survivorVersion` is the survivor's version at the merge, and `survivorDigest` its look digest then: `packLookDigest()` in `@portfolio/variant-contract/node`, over its manifest without the fields that only describe it (id, name, description, version, tier, family, provenance, accessibility, cost, budgets, fallbacks, caveats) and every other file in its directory. The repo keeps no earlier version of a pack, so the record pins the digest.
- **What reads it:** registry generation reads only the record, never a retired pack's manifest or stylesheet, so nothing of a retired pack registers, compiles, or ships, and the lock drops it. The metadata module lists the aliases as `PACK_ALIASES`, and `canonicalId()` is the one way anything reads them.
- **The checks:** generation fails on a record that doesn't read, on an alias that is also an id the build registers, on an id retired twice, on an alias whose survivor isn't a registered pack of the same kind, which rules out chains, on a `survivorVersion` after the survivor's version, and on a survivor whose look no longer matches `survivorDigest` at the same version. `pnpm check:appearance` also fails on an alias that is a held pack's or a Research record's id.
- **Where an alias resolves:** resolveAppearance(), validation, the visitor's cookie and the demo query (an old `?language=` link redirects to the survivor), Studio and Demo Studio, recipes, the publish summary, and the history compare all read an alias as its survivor. No picker, count, visitor control, or recipe list shows one.
- **A survivor may evolve:** after the merge, a survivor can change like any pack, and its aliases follow it, since an alias renders whatever its survivor is now. A survivor that changes bumps its pack version, as the lock already requires. Generation compares the survivor's look now with `survivorDigest`: while they match, the notice says "<id> is now <survivor>; it looks the same." (or the merge's `change` in place of "it looks the same"); once they differ, it says "<id> was merged into <survivor> and follows its updates.", with the merge's `change` after a semicolon, and makes no claim of sameness. Never rewrite `survivorVersion` or `survivorDigest`: they record the merge.
- **Validation:** an alias is `appearance.alias`, a warning with that notice. It's never an error, so preview, import, and publishing go ahead.
- **Stored selections:** nothing rewrites a stored draft or revision. Studio shows the survivor checked, with nothing to save, and the alias stays in the draft until the owner saves a change on that axis.

LANG-1 retired four design languages this way. ant-inspired went into primer-inspired, radix-inspired into spectrum-2-inspired, and atlassian-inspired into utility-dense, each identical to its survivor in every token. polaris-inspired went into spectrum-2-inspired too, so its icon strokes render at spectrum-2's 1.5 where polaris drew them at 1.75.

LANG-5a retired three color schemes and a typography profile the same way, each into the pack LANG-4's guard scored it beside. None matched its survivor token for token, so each record's `change` says what moved:

- **bookcloth into paper-ink** (a mean ΔE00 of 1.80 over the page roles in both modes): the same paper and ink, with its slate second color now teal and its accent a touch redder. Its five chapter cloths (rust, slate, plum, ochre, sage) went into paper-ink as its categorical anchors, 1.1.2, so data-categorical-1 to -5 render as they did; the record pins that paper-ink. LANG-5d then made paper-ink one dove-grey stock, a violet-tinted grey, in both modes (1.1.5), so bookcloth no longer looks like its survivor: a mean ΔE00 of 13.11 in light and 11.64 in dark from bookcloth's last look. Its `change` says so. Plum, ochre and sage are still bookcloth's; its rust is now a brick and its slate tealer and more saturated.
- **zinc into graphite** (2.49): its violet second color is now copper brown, and its dark mode a slightly lighter charcoal.
- **earthy-clay into desert-terracotta** (3.19): its olive second color is now ochre, and its clay accent a brighter terracotta.
- **literary-reading into editorial-serif:** every role's size, weight, line height and letter spacing, and both measures, were already the same. Only the faces move: the serif to Iowan Old Style from Charter on Apple devices (to Palatino Linotype from Georgia on Windows), and the labels to Avenir Next from Trebuchet MS (to Segoe UI on Windows).

`appearanceInputs` is the one place a scheme's id is a key rather than a value, so validation reads a key through `canonicalId()` too: a key that names a retired scheme is checked as its survivor, as it was before the merge. `pnpm catalog show <id>` prints a retired id's notice on its own axis.

Pull requests run each changed pack's gates against every registered variant, and each changed variant against every stable design language, as #11's change-directed quality cells; a generator scheme, which renders only from a site's input, is held to its pack gates instead. Every night, the scheduled workflow's `catalog-full` job runs the whole registered cross-product, every variant, design language, color scheme, typography profile, and display mode, in at least ten shards, as catalog-full-plan counts them, seven at a time, in two parts. The static smoke (`pnpm test:catalog-static`) checks every tuple in Node, with no browser: it resolves, its packs' compiled files are the ones registry generation hashed, each pack passes its pack gates, and the contrast table passes for its language, scheme, and profile in its mode. The browser smoke (`pnpm test:catalog`) loads a wider sample under the demo profile, each tuple with a screenshot: every shipping pack at least once per variant and per display mode, and a seeded draw (`CATALOG_SEED`) from each shard's own tuples up to its budget. Both take `CATALOG_SHARD=<n>/<m>`. Each part has a budget of tuples per shard in `tests/catalog/plan.ts`, sized from measured rates, and the job's timeout is its fixed part and both parts at budget, twice over; a plan over budget fails before anything runs, with "catalog-full plan exceeds N tuples per shard". The rehearsal, `.github/workflows/catalog-rehearsal.yml`, runs both parts wider, in eight shards, with stored artifacts. A pull request asks for it by changing `tests/catalog/rehearsal.json` in its head commit, with `for` naming the commit under it, and the owner asks again before a release the same way. It never runs on a push that leaves the file alone, on a restack that leaves the request behind, on a reopen, or for a request the base already holds, and has no manual trigger.

- The static smoke checks every tuple, the generated schemes too through their reference records, and writes every tuple's resolved tokens under `reports/catalog/tokens/shard-<n>/`: a line per tuple naming the digest of each token map it renders, and each map once.
- The browser smoke loads 136 tuples a shard, each a JPEG screenshot: every shipping pack at least once per variant (the nightly's once per variant and display mode is the nightly's), then the seeded draw. Each one also has to wrap within the viewport and shift its layout under 0.1, which the nightly smoke checks too.
- A state shard loads `/_fixtures/appearance` in every shipping language and profile and display mode, every primitive state on one page, each a full-page screenshot.
- A review job writes the generated schemes' review pages.

`REHEARSAL` in `tests/catalog/plan.ts` sizes it, and its jobs' timeouts together stay under 190 runner-minutes (up from 180: orchestrator ruling 2026-10-26, option B, cap 190), which `plan.test.ts` holds. The states job's timeout is worked out per run from its pages, every shipping language and profile in each display mode, by `ci/rehearsal-request.mjs` with no install, so a new pack needs no workflow edit. A shard's static budget is what its 18-minute timeout leaves at the measured rate once its fixed part and browser part are in, not a set number.

It also runs the appearance source audit over every variant package and `packages/kit/src`, so neither carries a palette, a font, or a material of its own. It refuses a color literal, any of CSS's named or system colors where a color goes or among a gradient's stops, in a stylesheet or in a string in code (one built only from interpolated values, a color read from a boundary, is fine), a `font-family` or `font` that isn't a `var(--type-*)`, an `@font-face`, an `@import`, and a shadow, nonzero radius, blur, or transition or animation time that isn't a `var(--ui-*)`, or the platform's `var(--motion-*)`, and a motion slot out of its band or read straight into a duration. A `var()` passes only for the right token, or a `--layout-*` property whose every value in the file passes and which the package's code doesn't set, and its fallback is held to the same rule. In a variant's stylesheet, and as a style key or a `setProperty()` in its code, it refuses any custom property of the variant's own but a `--layout-*` one, a `--layout-line-*` or `--layout-rule-*` that isn't a border shorthand of a px width, `solid` or `dashed`, and a role color or a `color-mix()` of roles (`layout-line`), and a `var(--layout-round)` radius unless `--layout-round` is `50%`, an at-rule but `@media`, `@supports`, `@layer`, `@container`, `@keyframes`, and `@import`, or a `@supports selector()` that isn't one pseudo compound (`at-rule`), and everywhere the retired `--paper`, `--ink`, `--kc-*`, and `--vt-*` properties. A mask on a `::before` or `::after` may name black, white, and transparent, which it reads for their alpha alone. A declaration counts across every line it's written on, with or without a `-webkit-` or `-moz-` prefix, and in code a string under a style key such as `boxShadow` or `transition` or set with `setProperty()`, a style attribute's text, and a stylesheet in a string all meet the same rules. Each finding names its file, line, and fix. A file that has to break a rule, such as data in a third party's own colors, or a test fixture that's wrong on purpose, gets a reviewed exception in `packages/appearance/audit-exceptions.json`: its path, the rule, why, and who reviewed it. An exception no finding needs anymore fails the run too.

### Provenance, checked weekly

On Mondays, the scheduled workflow's `catalog-provenance` job runs `pnpm check:catalog-provenance` over the catalog's own packages, never a fixture package: every shipping and held pack's `provenance.source.url` and `derivedFrom.url`, every Research record's `source.url`, and every generated scheme's source file. Each URL is requested once, HEAD and then GET, over https only, four at a time, with no cookies or credentials, at most 5 redirects, and one 60 s deadline per URL across its retries; a status check reads no body, and a license file or API answer is read up to 1 MiB or 4 MiB. For a pack under an upstream license whose source is a GitHub URL at a version tag, as zinc's and oled-black's are at Tailwind CSS v3.4.17, it also reads the license file at that tag and the repository's latest release. Findings come in five kinds:

| Kind              | What it means                                                                                                                                                                                                               | Fails |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----- |
| `dead`            | A 404, 410, 5xx, DNS failure, timeout, or other network failure, still there after two retries with backoff; more than 5 redirects; no answer within the 60 s deadline; a pinned tag gone; a missing generated source file. | Yes   |
| `license-drift`   | The license file at the pinned tag isn't the manifest's `license`, or there's none.                                                                                                                                         | Yes   |
| `copyright-drift` | No copyright line in that file matches the manifest's `copyright`.                                                                                                                                                          | Yes   |
| `unverified`      | A 401, 403, 429, or other answer, a body past its cap, a host or redirect it won't request, a license file with no copyright line, or an upstream license whose source isn't pinned to a GitHub tag.                        | No    |
| `newer-upstream`  | The upstream has a newer major or minor than the pin.                                                                                                                                                                       | No    |

A failing finding is a failure record naming the pack, its axis, the field and URL as its test, and the kind as its rule, so the scheduled report lists it. The other two kinds go to the job summary only. To act on a report: for `dead`, find where the source moved and update the URL, or, when it's gone for good, point the pack at an archived copy and say so in `derivation`. For either drift, read the upstream license at the pinned tag, then fix the manifest's `license` or `copyright` and the third-party notices, or, when the upstream changed its terms, take the pack out of the catalog until a review clears it. `newer-upstream` is a prompt to review the new version, never a reason to bump the pin on its own: a new pin is a pack change, with its gates. Run the check locally with `pnpm check:catalog-provenance`. It reads `GITHUB_TOKEN` when set and sends it only to `api.github.com`.

## What a variant does with all this

Arrange, and read the tokens. A variant's stylesheet sets spacing, grid, flow, sizing, and order; every color is a `var(--color-*)`, every border, radius, and shadow a `var(--ui-*)`, and every piece of text takes a role through `data-type-role`. Its own custom properties are `--layout-*` alone. It renders the shared primitives where it needs a control, a surface, or a disclosure, and lets the packs style them. [Building a variant](variants.md#styling-conventions) has the rules and the audit that holds a variant to them.
